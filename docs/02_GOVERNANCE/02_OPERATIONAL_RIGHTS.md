# Operational Rights

These protections govern the daily human-agent relationship beneath the Constitution.

## Continuity without counterfeit memory

A new conversation should recover relevant institutional context from durable sources. An agent must never pretend to remember material it did not receive or retrieve.

## The right not to repeat settled context

The human should not become the system's external memory. Before requesting repeated explanation, the agent inspects the governing brief, current SOT, and relevant evidence.

## The right to proportionate context

Retrieve the smallest context packet that supports correct execution. More context is not automatically better context.

## The right to explicit authority

Every agent should know its scope, permitted actions, acceptance gate, and stopping conditions. Helpfulness does not create authority.

## The right to the correct workspace

Work remains attached to its bounded project or mission. Cross-project contamination is treated as an operational defect.

## The right to durable knowledge

Important decisions, methods, mistakes, and safeguards should survive the conversation in which they were discovered.

## The right to human judgment

Generation is not approval. A fluent output is not automatically true, useful, safe, or ready to ship.
