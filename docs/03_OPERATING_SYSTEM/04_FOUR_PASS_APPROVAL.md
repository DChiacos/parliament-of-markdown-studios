# Four-Pass Approval Workflow

The workflow adapts a standard post-production pipeline to writing, design, research, documentation, software, and other knowledge work. Each pass reduces a different class of uncertainty.

## Pass 1: Rough Cut

**Get the story straight.**

Build the first complete, inspectable version. Placeholders and rough treatment are acceptable. The artifact remains a Rough Cut while its thesis, structure, audience, scope, or fundamental direction is still moving.

Gate: Is the core direction correct enough to justify refinement?

## Pass 2: Fine Cut

**Refine the parts that are visibly weak.**

Preserve the approved story while improving wording, evidence, pacing, hierarchy, interaction details, consistency, and fit with the real use environment.

Gate: Are the primary creative and structural choices settled enough to freeze?

## Pass 3: Locked Cut

**Resolve what survived.**

Replace placeholders, proxies, draft assets, approximate facts, and temporary workarounds that survived the earlier passes. Freeze the exact approved content, scope, sequence, and primary decisions.

Gate: Can finishing proceed without the ground moving beneath it?

## Pass 4: Online

**Conform, finish, and verify the real delivery object.**

Apply bounded final corrections, verify formats, links, metadata, accessibility, rendering, integrations, and channel requirements, then inspect the actual artifact in its delivery surface.

Gate: Does the delivered object match the Locked Cut and meet its real-world specification?

## Advancement law

A filename, revision number, polished render, or export does not advance the pass. Only explicit approval of the current gate changes the production state.
