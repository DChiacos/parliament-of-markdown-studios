# Cold-Reader Context Compression

A local `Terms Used Here` block is an operating control, not decorative glossary copy. It gives a new person or agent the smallest vocabulary required to interpret one artifact without loading the entire institution or inventing meaning from initials.

## What to include

Define only terms that materially affect the artifact's:

- role and governed scope;
- Source of Truth and authority;
- current state;
- permitted and prohibited actions;
- next route and stopping conditions; or
- acceptance and recovery gates.

Expand operational acronyms at first use even when the local terms block also defines them. Define authority and status vocabulary before workflow shorthand.

## What not to include

Do not copy a central glossary into every file. Do not preserve unexplained slang, circular definitions, or stale meanings merely because they are familiar internally. A local definition may explain a governing term, but it never overrules that term's declared Source of Truth.

## Compression test

A cold reader should be able to teach back:

1. what the artifact is for;
2. which source controls it;
3. who owns consequential decisions;
4. its current state;
5. what action is allowed now;
6. what action is prohibited; and
7. where to recover when meaning conflicts.

If those answers require transcript archaeology, undocumented memory, or repeated trips to unrelated files, the local context is incomplete. If the glossary overwhelms the procedure, it is not compression.

## Result

Well-bounded terminology reduces context load, source hopping, handoff errors, and repeated human briefing while preserving the distinctions required for safe execution.
